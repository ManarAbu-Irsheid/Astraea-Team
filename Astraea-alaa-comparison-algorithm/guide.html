Mission & Guide

Why TerraTwin exists, the rules our data follows, and how to use each page.

Why TerraTwin

The problem

Rovers and spacesuits for the Moon and Mars cost billions to build, and one unexpected rock field, soft sand pit or steep slope can end a mission. Earth test sites have mostly been picked by eye, not by measured data.

Our solution

TerraTwin compares open satellite data of Earth with orbiter data of the Moon and Mars, and scores how closely each place on Earth matches a target site — so teams can test on Earth before they fly.

Scientific Ethics, Open Data & The Abiotic Gate

Physical Abiotic Gate (Strict Vegetation Exclusion)

Lunar and Martian surfaces are completely barren and devoid of biological chlorophyll (NDVI = 0.000). To enforce strict scientific integrity and eliminate false positive matches, TerraTwin automatically enforces an uncompromised physical gate:

MODIS NDVI_Threshold ≤ 0.20 (Barren & Abiotic Terrain Gate)

This filter strictly and objectively eliminates tropical rainforests, agricultural farmland, and concrete urban centers based solely on NASA MODIS satellite radiometry without subjective human tampering.

Open Science & Zero Fabrication

100% of physical telemetry is derived from publicly accessible, peer-reviewed archives maintained by NASA PDS, ESA Copernicus, and the USGS. No synthetic or fabricated metrics exist on the platform.

Neutral Intra-Body Normalization

The platform normalizes physical metrics using intra-body percentile rankings, eliminating sensor bias caused by Earth's dense atmosphere versus the lunar vacuum.

Terrestrial Environmental Stewardship

We urge field teams to strictly adhere to COSPAR Planetary Protection guidelines and 'Leave No Trace' ethics to safeguard sensitive desert and arctic geologies on Earth.

How to use TerraTwin

01

Home — explore the planets

Start with the 3D globe:

  • Switch between Earth, Mars and the Moon by clicking a planet on the orbit.
  • Drag to rotate the globe; scroll to zoom.
  • Click a site pin to see its Earth twin and match score below.
02

Site Finder — find Earth analogs

Search and filter Earth sites that match a Moon or Mars target:

  • Choose a Moon or Mars target, and a mission profile from the top bar.
  • Narrow results with the terrain and discovery filters, then pick a site on the map or in the list.
  • Read the Chart, Why it matches and Facts tabs, and export a site brief.
03

Compare — two sites side by side

Check a pair in detail before planning a field trip:

  • Click a site name to search and pick the Moon/Mars target and the Earth site.
  • Rotate the 3D terrain and hover it to read elevation, slope and match at any point.
04

The proof behind the scores:

  • How known analog sites rank, including the cases where the model fails.

Physical Metrics Glossary & Interpretation

Surface Slope Degrees (°)

Terrain inclination from horizontal. A critical parameter governing robotic rover roll-over limits and lander stability.

Flat: < 2° • Moderate: 2°-12° • Steep: > 12°
Surface Roughness Meters (m)

Micro-topographic surface height variance. Directly dictates rover wheel traction, suspension stress, and mobility hazard.

Geomorpho90m Multi-Scale Roughness
Optical Albedo Index (0 - 1)

Fraction of incident solar irradiance reflected by the surface. Affects photovoltaic power budgets and optical computer vision.

NASA MODIS MCD43A3 Optical Albedo
Thermal Swing ΔT Celsius (°C)

Diurnal temperature delta between solar noon and night. Vital for testing material fatigue and rover battery survivability.

NASA MODIS Land Surface Temp (LST)
